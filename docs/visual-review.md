# Visual delivery review for the MVP

Research date: 25 September 2026. This details the visual portion of the [MVP research](mvp-research.md): posture, body movement, hand gestures, power sphere, and facial expression. The five videos currently in `videos/` teach vocal foundations only. Vinh's visual lessons should be added and reviewed before treating the rules below as his complete method.

## Coaching basis and first scope

Vinh's [Stage Workshop description](https://vinhgiang.com/programs/stage-workshop) names posture, gestures, movement, eye contact, and facial expressions as the visual side of communication. His [public body-language article](https://vinhgiang.com/blog/5-body-language-tips-to-become-more-charismatic) defines the **power sphere** as the space between the belly button and eyes for hand gestures, and teaches facial congruence with the words and gestures that help tell a story. This is enough to form testable provisional rules, but the actual lessons may add exceptions and demonstrations.

Start with one visible speaker facing a fixed phone or webcam, upper body and hands in frame, recorded for 30 seconds to 5 minutes. The first video prototype should review voice and visual delivery on a **single synchronized timeline**. Audio-only uploads still receive voice feedback. If hands, torso, or face are not visible, omit that category and explain the framing needed for another take. A creator's cropped headshot, a standing full-body talk, and a seated conversation have different observable regions; do not apply one posture or gesture threshold to all three.

| Dimension | Observable signal | Useful provisional feedback | Withhold when |
|---|---|---|---|
| Posture | Head, shoulders, and torso alignment and changes over time | “Your shoulders drop during this explanation; compare the two takes” when consistently visible and corroborated by a reviewer | Camera tilt, leaning by choice, cropped torso, disability or mobility differences, or ambiguous context |
| Body movement | Position and motion relative to the speaker's own baseline and frame | Point to a movement that competes with a key line, or a still moment that gives it space | Camera movement, cuts, off-screen movement, or an intentional expressive choice |
| Hand gestures | Hand visibility, range, repetition, and timing with emphasized words | Identify a gesture that illustrates a point or repeated movement that draws attention from it | Hands leave frame, tracking fails, or gesturing style is simply different |
| Power sphere | Visible wrist/palm positions relative to eyes and an estimated torso region | Show an overlay of where hands were during a selected phrase and invite a second take using the coached range | Eye or lower-torso boundary is missing, body is rotated, or the rule's contextual exception is unknown |
| Facial expression | Visible changes in brows, mouth, and head position, checked against spoken meaning and tone | Offer a tentative “does this expression support the warmth you intended here?” suggestion | Face is obscured, lighting poor, interpretation is uncertain, or the delivery is culturally/contextually appropriate |

These are coaching observations, not diagnoses or measures of someone's inner confidence, sincerity, or personality. In particular, a neutral face, a different posture, or gesturing outside the power sphere is not automatically a defect. The user can mark a choice as intentional. Avoid percentage “eye contact” claims from camera gaze unless the recording setup and measurement have been validated; looking at a lens is not the same task as looking at people in a room.

## Feasible technical path

1. **Preserve the original timeline.** Decode sound with FFmpeg and sample video frames with original timestamps. Detect cuts, camera motion, resolution, blur, exposure, and which body parts remain visible. Match every visual event to the same timeline used for transcription and audio features.
2. **Track visible geometry locally.** [MediaPipe Pose Landmarker](https://developers.google.com/edge/mediapipe/solutions/vision/pose_landmarker) locates body landmarks in video frames; [Hand Landmarker](https://developers.google.com/edge/mediapipe/solutions/vision/hand_landmarker) locates hands; [Face Landmarker](https://developers.google.com/edge/mediapipe/solutions/vision/face_landmarker) can provide facial landmarks and optional expression coefficients. These produce coordinates and motion signals, not coaching judgments. Track missing detections rather than interpolating long gaps. Normalize distances to the speaker's visible body dimensions, not pixels alone.
3. **Use a synchronized multimodal review.** [Gemini video understanding](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/video-understanding) accepts video with audio and timestamped questions. Its default fixed sampling is 1 frame per second, which can miss quick gestures. Inspect selected moments at a higher local frame rate, or supply short video clips at a suitable rate. The model can judge whether a visible event supports the spoken point, but exact measurements and timestamps should come from the pipeline.
4. **Apply Vinh's rubric.** Represent each lesson as a rule, rationale, examples, exceptions, and source timestamp. The five local voice lessons can already be converted this way. Add Vinh's visual lesson clips and timestamped demonstrations before finalizing posture, gesture, power-sphere, or expression rules. His public article provides only a provisional power-sphere definition.
5. **Show evidence and a retry.** Display the timestamped clip, a simple optional landmark/gesture overlay, one specific suggestion, and a second-take comparison. Keep the maximum feedback count across voice and visuals at three priorities total initially; users should not receive a pile of competing corrections.

The power sphere is a **coaching region**, not a universal detection label. Eyes are visible in many shots, but the belly button may not be. For an upper-body shot, estimate a lower torso boundary only when the relevant landmarks are visible and validated. If it is not, describe hand height relative to visible shoulders or chest without claiming an exact power-sphere violation. First measure how often a hand can be tracked at all; then ask human reviewers whether a flagged moment merits feedback.

## Validation and scope impact

The existing voice feasibility results do not establish visual accuracy. Build a separate small set of consented clips with varied framing, lighting, skin tones, clothing, body shapes, movement abilities, and speaking styles. Have Vinh or trained reviewers mark which visible moments deserve a suggestion and which are acceptable choices. Include deliberate examples: a useful illustrative gesture, hands outside the coached range by choice, a face that suits a serious line, and camera motion mistaken for body movement. Report precision, false criticism rate, and coverage separately for each visual dimension and recording setup. A model that withholds all visual feedback should not count as successful.

The fastest technical smoke test is a 20–30-second uncut video with a clear upper body and hands. Run landmark tracking, render the overlay, and send the synchronized clip plus the provisional rule to a video-capable model. Ask a human whether the best one or two suggestions are accurate and useful. This tests feasibility; it does not validate the product. The audio-only engineering estimate and cost in [mvp-research.md](mvp-research.md) are **not estimates for this combined product**. Video processing, human review, and UI work add time and cost. Google's [video token documentation](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/video-understanding) gives about 100 input tokens per second at low-resolution static processing and about 300 at high resolution, before prompt/output and separate transcription; actual billed usage and processing time should be measured on the target clips.

## Competitive implication

Visual analysis itself is established: [Yoodli describes](https://yoodli.ai/our-partnerships/yoodli-powered-by-google-cloud) video feedback for eye contact, gestures, and facial expression alongside vocal feedback. The potential product advantage is a faithful implementation of Vinh's visual and vocal teaching with fair, specific feedback on an individual's own clip. That must be demonstrated in blind comparisons, not assumed from the lesson library or branding.
